Shift & Oversight Coverage

The port runs a 24h cycle. Traffic volume, which disruptions are plausible, and how much gets recorded all change with the shift. Counts below measure what was observed, not what happened -- a quiet night is a coverage artefact before it is anything else.

Sites & Observation Bias

Where a movement is decides how much of what happens to it gets written down, the same way the shift does. A gatehouse reconciles every movement; a remote depot reconciles in batch the next morning. So the counts below are counts of records, and ranking sites by them puts the best-watched site at the top. Both orderings are shown; neither is a risk ranking, for the reasons in the third card.

Fraud Network View

Entities that have co-occurred in the same case. A connection means "appeared together in a case," not an accusation -- click a node for detail, then "Trace path from here" and a second node to see the shortest chain of shared cases between them. Every entity in a case is linked to every other, so a dense little cluster from one case is guaranteed by the construction and means nothing on its own -- the structure list says which groups actually span several cases. Sites (grey, outer ring) are drawn for orientation and excluded from that analysis: every movement passes through a handful of gates and yards, so a site connects to nearly everything whatever is happening.

Analyst Calibration

A mirror, not a score. Cases you closed are checked against what this simulation actually recorded about them -- not against the real world, and never to tell you that you were right or wrong.

Exposure & Cost Model

Investigative hours are measured by this simulation. The rate that turns them into money, and the value of the cargo, are stated assumptions. Expected loss and loss avoided are refused — the third card explains why, and it is there at the same weight as the other two on purpose.

Portfolio Analytics

Aggregation only — nothing on this panel is measured here, and every figure is reported against the base it was actually computed over. Percentages on a dashboard look alike and are not: some are counts over a sample of closures, and the amber ones are numbers stated in a model file, which cannot move with the data at all. Counts stay visible beside every percentage, and a rate whose base is too small to divide stays a count.
